Engine 01 · Workout

Reads your cabinet. Writes your day.

Reads your active cabinet. Filters exercises by pharma caution tags. Adjusts session length and volume by your check-in scores AND active compounds. Suggests intent based on yesterday's RPE.

  • 128 exercises tagged for GLP-1 nausea, beta-blocker HR cap, cortisol spike sensitivity
  • Auto-generates a session based on your split, days/week, and active compounds
  • Adapts in real-time as your cabinet changes

Why this matters

A GLP-1 user shouldn't be programmed for a high-volume nausea-trigger session. A beta-blocker user shouldn't be programmed for sprint intervals at 95% HR max. The engine knows.

Engine 02 · Nutrition

TDEE with per-compound modifiers.

Computes TDEE with per-compound modifiers. T3 +12%. Beta-blockers −6%. Clenbuterol +9%. GLP-1 modifies appetite signal independently. Macros auto-adjust as you add or remove compounds.

  • Mifflin-St Jeor baseline with activity multiplier
  • Pharma TDEE modifiers per published research
  • Appetite-floor logic for GLP-1 users (suggests minimum protein even when appetite-suppressed)
T3 +12% Beta-blockers −6% Clen +9% GLP-1 appetite

Why this matters

A Semaglutide user who eats to appetite loses muscle. A T3 user who eats baseline TDEE crashes. The math knows.

Engine 03 · Pharmacokinetics

Single-compartment. C = D/V × e−ke·t

Standard single-compartment model. Models when each compound peaks and troughs. Overlays multiple compounds on one chart to show when the full stack peaks simultaneously.

  • Half-life data per compound from published research
  • Educational approximation (clearly disclaimed in-app)
  • Visualizes interaction windows for stacked compounds

Why this matters

Knowing your BPC-157 peaks at hour 4 and your TB-500 peaks at hour 16 changes when you train, when you dose, and when you stack. The math knows.

The corpus

100+ compounds. Every claim cited.

The corpus is a structured research database with published dose ranges, cycle ranges, safety flags, expected lab marker movement, sex-specific guidance, evidence quality grading, and WADA-banned status. AI is required to cite a corpus entry for any compound-related claim. Out-of-corpus → flagged. Out-of-range → flagged.

What the AI does — and what it doesn't

A clear line. Drawn on purpose.

✓ Does

  • Generates workout programming from your stack
  • Sets macro numbers on the engine's TDEE math
  • Interprets bloodwork against corpus-expected response
  • Coaches via citation and synthesizes the daily brief
  • Flags out-of-corpus claims and stops itself

✗ Never does

  • Recommends prescription dose changes
  • Diagnoses conditions
  • Invents numbers the corpus can't support
  • Says "consult your doctor" as an escape hatch

Educational tool only. Not a substitute for medical advice. Always confirm with your clinician before changing your protocol.
